Will the U.S. confirm that aliens exist?

Question Model Background

GitHub · suggest a change · how to contribute

Base case

Throughout human history the answer has been no: there is speculation, rumor and belief, but no confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial life. The base case is that the answer continues to be no. The model adjusts from there.

Probability over time

This is a discrete event with an increasing probability of happening the further out we look. For a scientific find, I settled on a logistic curve.

The curve is flat at first, because current leads need time to mature and new ones to emerge. It steepens as observation and detection technology advances, then levels off at the probability that life can be found at all.

When it steepens, and how fast, are inputs in the model below.

Three paths to Yes

Discovering aliens and the U.S. saying so are different events. The market needs the statement (rules). The model accounts for three ways to get one: science, disclosure, and an off-the-cuff remark. Any route to a statement should fall into one of the three.

Yes: a U.S. official or agency states definitively that extraterrestrial life exists.

Science

A confirmed find, stated by a U.S. agency. The most likely path.

  • NASA research
  • Research abroad, once a U.S. official restates it
  • Direct contact or a visit

Leads: Mars, other worlds, other samples. Search for life

Disclosure

The government already holds proof and chooses to confirm it.

Government disclosure

Off-the-cuff remark

An official says aliens are real with more confidence than the evidence supports, and the exchange counts it. A tail risk.

The paths are treated as independent: chance of Yes = 1 − (1 − Science) × (1 − Disclosure) × (1 − Remark).

Against the market

Assumptions
  • A concrete discovery would be publicized. It would not be held up in scientific peer review.
  • The three paths are independent. At first glance, yes; this is open to further evaluation.
  • For disclosure and for a remark, probability accrues at a constant rate from 2026-11-05 to the end of the presidential term, Jan 20, 2029. Nothing accrues after that. The chart is cumulative, so both stay level from then on.
Weak points
  • The near-term science curve may be too flat. Most of the gap with the market is here.
  • Insider trading. This market is prone to insider trading by informed actors aware of an upcoming announcement, which skews the tail risk toward Yes.
  • "Definitively" is undefined. A confident remark could count without proof.4
  • Discovery is not announcement. Scientists take years and hedge their language.
What would change the forecast
  • New biosignature news: Mars, K2-18b, any sample return.
  • A new PURSUE file release, or a White House or agency statement on UAP.
  • Kalshi clarifying the rules or resolving a borderline case.
  • New monthly markets listing (Feb 1, Mar 1, …), which add markets to the term structure.
Open questions
  • What does the path from a discovery to an official announcement look like in practice: a press conference, or years of peer review?
  • What do surveys of scientists say about whether life exists elsewhere, and when it will be found?
  • Should known milestones, like a sample return or a mission arrival, show up as steps on the curve?
  • Asteroid samples (Bennu, Ryugu) are not yet researched.
  • Would a confirmed sign of past life on Mars count? The contract terms say life "exists" and do not address life that once existed.4
  • If the government held proof, why would it not have been released already? What is the political, national security or strategic advantage in withholding it, when declassifying was campaign rhetoric?
